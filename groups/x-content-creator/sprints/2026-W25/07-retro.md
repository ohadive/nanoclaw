## Sprint Arc

The sprint built one argument across five formats:

```
Thread (seed) → X Article (centerpiece) → QT × 3 (distribution) → Infographic (visual proof)
```

The thread planted the frame on Jun 17. The article on Jun 26 was the full argument. The QTs spent the last week pointing back to it from three different entry angles. The infographic closed the week by making the abstract concrete — five near-identical review-app listings in search results, one that reads in a second.

---

## What Landed

- **The frame held across five formats.** "Confidence gap, not options gap" worked as a standalone thread hook, an article premise, three distinct QT angles, and a visual. That kind of coherence doesn't happen every sprint.
- **QT repointing worked.** Shifting the QTs to quote the article (rather than the thread) gives the long-form a longer tail and is worth repeating. Each QT is a re-entry point to the same piece.
- **Three distinct QT angles from one article.** Confidence vs. options / pre-click decision surface / committing to what you don't do — none of these overlap. The article had enough density to support it.
- **Infographic as closer.** Ending the week with a visual after four text-heavy posts felt right. The "blur" metaphor is stronger as an image than as a description.

---

## What Didn't

- **Thin reach overall.** Thread root at 99 impressions, article at 47. Not a strategy failure — account is still small — but worth watching whether Pillar 3 / positioning content structurally underperforms vs. operational/AI-stack content.
- **Manual publishing created tracking gaps.** Thread and article both published manually, so impressions require a separate X analytics lookup. Next sprint: run everything through Typefully where possible.
- **No LinkedIn parallel.** The article format in particular would have translated. Didn't happen this sprint.

---

## Carry Forward

**Repeat:**
- One central argument, five formats, single sprint — this structure works
- QTs pointing back to long-form (not just thread) for distribution
- Close visual week on Thursday or Friday
- Keep QT angles non-overlapping

**Change:**
- Get thread and article into Typefully drafts so metrics land in one place
- Consider leading with the infographic on Wednesday (mid-sprint) rather than as a closer — could drive more eyes to the article while it's fresh
- "Pre-click decision surface" (QT-2 angle) is underdeveloped — strong enough for its own standalone thread next sprint

**Shelf for later:**
- A/B two article titles: one curiosity-led ("You're competing with 7"), one problem-led ("Why confidence beats choice in app discovery")
- Explore whether the blur visual could work as a Reels/short-form clip — static image version was clean
